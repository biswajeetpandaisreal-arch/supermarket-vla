\begin{abstract}
Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models promise generalist robot control by
extending a pretrained vision--language backbone to output robot actions,
letting a single network handle perception, language understanding and
manipulation together. However, state-of-the-art VLAs are large, raising the
question of whether a capable VLA can be fine-tuned and deployed on a single
consumer GPU. This thesis investigates that question through a language-driven
supermarket manipulation task, built in the MuJoCo physics simulator, in which
a UR10e arm on a stationary Omron mobile base must pick one of four named grocery
items from a shelf and place it in an onboard basket. A preliminary study on the RoboCasa kitchen
benchmark found that small, deployable models (SmolVLA and ACT) failed even after
fine-tuning on RoboCasa demonstrations, motivating a controlled task and a
methodology-first approach. The
compact SmolVLA model is fine-tuned on 208 scripted-expert demonstrations by
freezing its pretrained vision--language backbone and training only the action
expert, which uses approximately 3~GB of a 12~GB GPU. For the selected checkpoint
the policy achieves an 80\% task-completion rate (95\% Wilson confidence interval
70--87, $n=80$) and 92.5\% grasp success, drawn from 320 closed-loop rollouts across
four checkpoints, with the deployed checkpoint selected by task success rather than
training loss. A documented failure analysis turned an initial 0\% placement rate
($n=12$) into 80\% by diagnosing near-miss behaviour and addressing observability and
episode-boundary issues rather than blindly retraining. A controlled test shows that
the policy is language-conditioned: changing only the named item changes where the arm
reaches. The work shows that careful evaluation and data quality matter more than
model scale at this regime, and that a capable VLA is deployable \emph{in simulation}
under tight hardware constraints.
\end{abstract}
